Solver_2D: a verified incompressible Navier-Stokes solver

Personal project · May 2026 – present · github.com/adiikumar/Solver_2D

A 2D incompressible Navier-Stokes solver on a staggered MAC grid, written from scratch. CFD is an unforgiving place to write code: a sign error still compiles, still runs, and still draws a plausible contour, because correctness lives in long-range consistency across files rather than in local syntax. I kept the codebase small enough that reviewing every line was literally possible, and built the solver with an AI agent under a spec, review and run gate to see where that workflow holds and where it needs checking.

FormulationPrimitive variables, staggered MAC grid, RK3 fractional step (Le & Moin, JCP 1991)
Pressure PoissonFFT with modified wavenumbers (periodic) · unpreconditioned CG (wall-bounded)
Discrete structureGradient built as minus the divergence transpose, so projection removes divergence exactly
SimplificationProjection applied at every RK3 substage, documented rather than silent
WorkflowSpec, generate, review, run. Human-gated throughout, with no autonomous execution
15/15unit tests passing, run locally
2nd orderMMS convergence, 4 grids

Conjugate heat transfer study of a liquid-cooled cold plate

Personal project · Apr – Aug 2026

A liquid-cooled microchannel cold plate under a 700 W heat load, the order a modern accelerator dissipates. The design question is a trade: narrower channels move heat better but cost pumping power, so the useful comparison holds pumping power fixed. I swept 96 geometries through a templated SLURM array pipeline, then fitted a Gaussian Process surrogate to the converged runs and searched the design space with it rather than with more CFD.

SolverOpenFOAM, chtMultiRegionSimpleFoam
Design sweep96 cases: 4 channel widths × 2 fin thicknesses × 3 channel heights × 4 flow rates
PipelineTemplated SLURM array jobs in Apptainer containers on a university HPC cluster
SurrogateGaussian Process, Matérn kernel (scikit-learn), constrained grid-search optimization
VerificationShah-London friction factor correlation, 0.37% error at the finest mesh; Grid Convergence Index study per Roache
89/96cases converged
22%thermal resistance cut, matched pumping power
2.21% / 0.06%GCI, pressure drop / thermal resistance
The surrogate does not get the last word. The Gaussian Process prediction at the optimum was confirmed against an independent CFD solve before being trusted. A surrogate is an interpolator, and the only way to know it has not drifted at the point you actually care about is to go back and run the solver there.

Marine CFD: submarine wake and buoy heave

Course project · STAR-CCM+

A two-part free-surface study. First, an overset mesh with volume-of-fluid capture resolves the wake of a DARPA SUBOFF hull travelling beneath the surface. Second, DFBI couples that wake to a floating cylindrical buoy and lets it heave freely, so the buoy becomes an instrument for the wake rather than just another body in the domain. Its heave amplitude tracks both its offset from the hull's path and the hull's speed.

GeometryDARPA SUBOFF bare hull (L 4.356 m, D 0.508 m) and a cylindrical buoy (D 1.0 m, H 4.0 m)
DomainSymmetry-halved rectangular domain; free-surface runs add a one-wavelength damping zone
MeshOverset: hull, buoy and background regions, roughly 4.5M cells
Boundary conditionsFroude-scaled velocity inlet, pressure outlet, slip symmetry walls, no-slip hull, VOF interface
Solver settingsURANS with elliptic blending Reynolds stress closure, VOF, DFBI overset motion, timestep 0.001 s

Formula Student aerodynamics

Aerodynamics lead · Veloce Racing · 2021 – 2023

Two seasons leading aerodynamics for the team's car, covering both the external package (bodywork, cockpit, main hoop, A-arms, wheels, wings) and the internal sidepod cooling duct. Meshing and case setup were scripted through PyFluent so a geometry change could be meshed, solved and post-processed overnight instead of over a week, which is what made a 30-configuration DOE affordable on a student timeline. The duct was run twice on purpose, with the radiator absent and installed, because only the second case shows whether it actually feeds the core.

Screw compressor: thermodynamic model and surrogate

Research co-op · Kirloskar Pneumatic · 2024 – 2025

A chamber-volume thermodynamic model of a twin-screw compressor, written in Python against CoolProp. The base model, with no leakage, overpredicted mass flow by around 66 g per cycle against vendor software, and closing that gap meant integrating six separate leakage paths. Once the model was trustworthy I compressed 1,000 runs with POD and fitted a Gaussian Process to map rotor geometry directly to the chamber-cycle response, turning a design study that needed a solve per candidate into one that needed a prediction.

DomainSingle working chamber against rotor crank angle (0–360°), a lumped thermodynamic model rather than 3D CFD
PhysicsSix leakage paths integrated against CoolProp property data
Training data1,000 runs × 700 crank-angle steps, forming the POD snapshot matrix
Dimensionality reductionTruncated SVD (POD), 3 to 8 modes typically capturing about 99% of variance
SurrogateGaussian Process, Matérn kernel (ν = 2.5) with a white-noise term for solver noise; inputs are L/D ratio, wrap angle, lobe combination and rotor speed, scaled on the training split only

GPU-accelerated DNS: pressure-Poisson solver port

Research Assistant · Computational Fluid Mechanics Lab, UW · 2025 – present

The lab's DNS solver for droplet-laden turbulent flows runs C/CUDA kernels behind Fortran host wrappers with MPI on the Hyak cluster. My work targets its pressure-Poisson solve, the most expensive step in the timeloop: first optimizing it on NVIDIA hardware, then porting that implementation to AMD GPUs using HIP and hipFFT.

CodebaseC/CUDA kernels, Fortran host wrappers, MPI, on the UW Hyak cluster
TargetThe pressure-Poisson solve, optimized on NVIDIA architectures
PortCUDA to AMD GPUs via HIP, cuFFT to hipFFT
VerificationTaylor-Green vortex regressions gate every change, alongside the lab's ptest operator suite
